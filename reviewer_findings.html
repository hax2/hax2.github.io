What survived the reviewers?

The modern-corpus audit strengthens WCS as a decoding diagnostic. It does not yet establish that reachability loss causes impoverished generated language. This page separates what the new evidence supports, what it overturns, and what remains in progress.

A narrower claim is now considerably stronger.

WCS cleanly measures whether an exact human-attested word remains reachable after a decoding filter. FineWeb shows that substantial exclusion persists in contemporary web contexts, so the original PG-19 distribution mismatch was not the sole explanation.

The evidence does not justify “censorship,” language suppression, cognitive harm, or a general claim that instruction tuning always erodes lexical access.

Reviewer-by-reviewer

Concern → evidence → verdict

The response should concede construct limits directly while emphasizing the modern-corpus replication and expanded parameter sweep.

bYax · Corpus mismatch

Addressed
Could PG-19 literary language be driving low reachability?

New evidence: 200 words, 50 FineWeb contexts each, all matched across six models. At top-k 50, 24.0–58.5% of exact word paths remain unreachable.

Verdict: The mismatch was a valid concern, but it does not explain away the phenomenon.

oHfT · Top-k range

Addressed
Was stopping at k=20 unrealistically restrictive?

New evidence: Increasing k from 20 to 80 raises WCS by roughly 14–15 percentage points across all six models.

Verdict: The reviewer was right. Extending to 80 materially changes absolute coverage and brackets common k=20, 50, and 64 configurations.

bYax · Statistical scale

Mostly addressed
Were 100 words × 10 contexts too unstable?

New evidence: The audit now contains 10,000 contexts per model with identical sample IDs and complete token paths.

Remaining: The paper still needs clustered confidence intervals and explicit paired significance reporting, rather than point estimates alone.

bYax / qqwR · Downstream validity

Running
Does WCS predict diversity in actual generations?

Old generation: Untruncated output only; it cannot test sampler-induced changes. Llama Instruct also became incoherent, inflating MTLD.

Replacement: Matched prompts, chat templates for Instruct models, normal EOS, fixed 100-word scoring, and varied top-k/top-p/min-p are now running.

bYax · Tokenizer confound

Analysis required
Do token counts distort cross-model WCS comparisons?

Observed: Single-token targets differ sharply: Gemma 91%, Llama 52%, Mistral 8%.

Verdict: The concern is real. The relationship is model-specific rather than simply monotonic, so token-count strata must be shown.

qqwR · Binary WCS

Scope limitation
Does 0/1 survival ignore probability degradation?

Our position: Binary WCS is exact for the narrow reachability question: possible or impossible after filtering.

Reviewer’s point: It cannot measure likelihood, semantic quality, or graded degradation. A probability-weighted companion should be presented separately.

qqwR · Normative language

Reviewer right
Do “censorship” and “lexical erasure” exceed the evidence?

Verdict: Yes. The evidence supports decoding-induced exclusion, reachability constraints, and lexical truncation—not intent, suppression, or social harm.

oHfT · Human-language premise

Reframe
Are human and model generation comparable enough for language-evolution claims?

Verdict: Not from these experiments. Reframe WCS as a debugging and evaluation tool; treat long-term language effects as a hypothesis requiring separate evidence.

Defensible claim

“Post-training changes word reachability, but its direction and magnitude depend on model family and decoding filter.”

Claim to remove

“Aligned or instruction-tuned models consistently exhibit greater lexical erosion than base models.” FineWeb does not support this generalization.

Active experiment

The missing causal link

The replacement run changes only the decoding condition within each model and measures whether WCS and realized lexical diversity move together while preserving coherent prompting.

Matched downstream validation · in progress

Six models × 50 shared contexts × two temperatures × twelve decoder conditions. Instruct models use native chat templates; generations stop normally at EOS; the first 100 lexical words are scored. Completion rate is reported alongside TTR and MTLD.

Why the earlier table is not evidence

It used only top-k=0 and top-p=1.0. With no varied filter, it cannot test whether truncation-induced changes in WCS predict changes in output diversity.

Why coherence matters

Llama Instruct’s mean MTLD of 389 versus 91 for Base was driven by pathological, nearly non-repeating output. Diversity without coherence is not linguistic quality.

Recommended framing

What the revised paper can say

Lead with the diagnostic contribution, concede the known tail-truncation mechanism, and keep downstream consequences conditional on the running validation.

“Tail truncation by decoding filters is well known. WCS contributes a context-sensitive, word-level audit of its consequences: it identifies which human-attested lexical paths remain reachable under a specific model and inference configuration. On 10,000 contemporary FineWeb contexts per model, substantial exact-word exclusion persists across commonly used decoding ranges. These results establish a decoding-layer reachability constraint; they do not by themselves establish semantic impoverishment, intentional suppression, or long-term language change.”
Suggested central claim for the abstract, introduction, and conclusion.
Evidence state: FineWeb forced-path audit complete; top-p summaries corrected to retain the nucleus boundary token; matched downstream generation still running. Do not merge preliminary downstream claims into the paper until all model/configuration outputs and correlation summaries are complete.
